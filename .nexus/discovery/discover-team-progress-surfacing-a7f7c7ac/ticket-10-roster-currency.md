## Question

What keeps a team's roster current, given that no act in the system requires anyone to
update it? [inferred]

The roster resolution made a declared roster the only source of the team view's rows, and it
gave the roster no forcing function. A roster can rot in two directions at once. Items that
finished months ago stay on it and print as unknown every week, so the view fills with rows
nobody is going to act on. Work that started last week is not on it, so the view is silent
about the thing most likely to be in trouble. Both councils named this as the roster's cost.

Decide whether Ripples does anything about it, and if so what. The candidates are: nothing,
because the roster is the team's to keep; a line in the weekly post naming items that have
been unknown for a long time and asking whether they are still live; a turn in the 1:1 that
asks a person about their own long-silent items; or Ripples noticing in a check-in that
someone is talking about work that is not on the roster and saying so. Each of those is a
message somebody receives, so each has to be paid for out of the conduct standard's message
budget, and the roster-item resolution already ruled that a check-in never adds or removes
an item.

## Why it blocks

A stub for building the team view can be written now, but a stub for keeping it worth reading
cannot. If Ripples does nothing, that is a product decision with a stated consequence and it
needs saying out loud rather than being left as an omission. If Ripples does something, the
something is a surface and a message, and neither is stated anywhere yet.
